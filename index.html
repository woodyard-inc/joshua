The approach

§ 01
From the desk of
Joshua Woodyard Now · in London

I studied marketing (BSc) and behavioural economics (MSc) because I wanted to understand why people, at scale, do what they do. That's shaped two methods I regularly draw on to reach conclusions.

The first is technical: years spent in SQL, Python, and dashboarding taught me that if you can't build the infrastructure to test an idea, it's not an insight — it's a hunch. The second is behavioural: formal training in decision-making and cognitive psychology taught me that even good infrastructure produces bad answers if you can't interpret why people actually behave the way they do.

Neither is sufficient alone. Technical skill without behavioural grounding produces dashboards and reports that are technically correct and practically useless — numbers nobody trusts enough to act on. Behavioural insight without technical rigor produces opinions dressed up as strategy. What I bring is the discipline to hold both: building something defensible, then reading it the way a decision-scientist would.

It's a combination I want to apply more directly — using robust infrastructure to answer harder questions about markets and behaviour, not just to maintain the systems that make those questions answerable.
