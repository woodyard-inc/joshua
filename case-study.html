The brief § 01

UNIQLO's New York merch team had a suspicion. Weekday-afternoon foot traffic was behaving oddly — the baskets looked smaller, the dwell-times longer, and the sell-through by category didn't match the weekend pattern they'd planned around.

They wanted a quick answer. I offered a slower one. Six weeks, forty stores, and a method that would survive scrutiny from both a buyer and a statistician. No deck at the end — a memo, a calendar, and a defensible decision.

We don't want research. We want a merch decision we can defend next quarter.

— Regional merchandising lead · week 1

Method § 02

I ran two streams in parallel. Quantitative: POS data cleaned and clustered by k-means on a 12-week pre-trend, giving me four behavioural clusters of store. Qualitative: consented observational fieldwork in a representative store from each cluster — shadowing forty-seven customers through their full visit, timing dwell, coding conversational snippets, and comparing basket against the weekend median.

The two streams checked each other. Where the clusters disagreed with the field notes, I went back to the store.

The finding § 03

What I found was less interesting than what I didn't find. The two cohorts — weekday afternoon vs. weekend — behaved almost identically in the first three minutes: same walls, same hesitations, same self-conscious glances at the mirror. The divergence came later, and it was emotional, not economic.

Weekday-afternoon shoppers were finishing a task — a meeting, an errand, a commute — and the store functioned as a decompression room. They dwelled longer but bought fewer items. Weekend shoppers were starting a social outing and bought companions' worth of things.

The merchandising implication was the opposite of what the team had assumed.

Recommendation § 04

Rather than merchandise the afternoon differently by category, merchandise it differently by pace. Fewer trend pieces on the front wall; more quiet essentials at adult eye level; one clearly-marked "something for the commute home" edit near checkout. The weekend plan stayed.

The team ran the new plan in three pilot stores the following quarter. Weekday-afternoon sell-through moved up nine percent against a matched-control set.

What I'd change § 05

Two things. First, I'd involve the store associates earlier — they saw the pattern before my clusters did, and I should have listened in week one instead of week four. Second, I'd ship a shorter memo; the long version was read by two people, and both of them had already agreed with me.

Method note K-means on 12-week pre-trend was the clustering that survived a leave-one-store-out robustness check. Two other specifications did not.
Tool stack R for clustering and visualisation. Tableau for the memo's live dashboard. Pen and notebook for the fieldwork.
What surprised me That the most useful finding was behavioural, not categorical — and that the buying team could act on "pace" as easily as they could act on "SKU."
Quietly proud of Shipping a recommendation the buyer could defend in front of her director without needing me in the room.
